The expected sale price is the main problem

Across every batch we can measure, the price actually achieved on Amazon came in below what the VA sheet expected 86% of the time — by an average of £7.80 a unit.

This is the root cause behind Section 1's numbers. A batch priced to sell at the VA's expected price would clear target ROI on schedule far more often. Instead, the price we actually get is consistently, not occasionally, below plan — which reads as a forecasting problem on the sheet, not a run of bad luck on individual ASINs.

Practically: today's Amazon price at the moment of sourcing is a poor predictor of the price several weeks later when stock actually lands and starts selling. The sheet needs to be pricing against something more stable — a 90-day average or a deliberately conservative haircut — rather than the live price on the day the lead is written up.

EU A2A is both our single largest source of purchases (42 batches, the 2nd biggest method) and one of the two weakest performers. A2A is worse still on ROI, though on smaller volume. Meanwhile Arbisource, Brand Sourcing and Online Arbitrage all clear target ROI comfortably — on much smaller volume. This isn't a case for abandoning EU A2A — it's still profitable on average, and it's a large share of our pipeline. That's worth a dedicated look before deciding how much of the buying budget it should keep.

08

What we'd change

In the order we'd tackle them — the first two are root causes; the rest compound on top of them.

Price against a 90-day average, not today's spot price

The VA sheet's expected sale price is the single biggest driver of the −33% profit miss (Section 2). Require a 90-day average or a fixed conservative discount off today's price before a lead is submitted, so the target it's measured against is realistic from day one.

Fixes the root cause behind Sections 1, 2 and 5 at once.

Record which VA lead every Buy Sheet order fulfils

Even just the lead's row number or submission date against the purchase order removes the ambiguity behind 48 of our 75 untrackable batches (Section 4). Without this, a fifth of everything we buy stays permanently unmeasurable.

This is pure record-keeping — no change to what we buy, only to whether we can tell if it worked.

Look hard at why EU A2A margins run thin

It's our 2nd-largest source of purchases and the weakest ROI (16.5%, Section 3). Before cutting volume, work out whether that's competition on the same routes, categories with structurally tight margins, or pricing sourced too close to the wire — then decide whether to fix it or reallocate buying budget toward Online Arbitrage, Brand Sourcing and Arbisource, all comfortably above target ROI on smaller volume.

Our biggest channel shouldn't also be our worst without a clear reason why.

Re-anchor the "Good" vs "Ok" rating against real outcomes

They currently predict nothing (Section 7). Walk back through a sample of each with him and agree what should actually separate a "Good" lead from an "Ok" one going forward — ideally tied to something measurable (sales rank stability, review count, price-drop depth) rather than impression.

A rating that doesn't discriminate outcomes isn't helping either of you prioritise.

Add a 60-day stock check, not just 90

All 10 dead-stock lines (Section 6) were rated Good or Ok and still didn't move. A review trigger at 60 days — price cut, bundle, or write off — would catch these a month earlier, while there's still room to recover margin instead of losing it entirely.

£2,163 is currently just sitting on a shelf with no plan.

Clean up the Sourcing Method values

"Keepa Product Finder" and "Keppa Product Finder" are both in the data as separate methods, and "Storefront Stalking" appears twice with slightly different text. Small thing, but it's why Section 3 needed cleaning before it could be trusted — a fixed dropdown instead of free text would keep it reliable without our having to re-normalise it every time.

Also fixes the weekly report, where "Sourcing method used" has come through blank for 100% of this week's 32 submissions.
