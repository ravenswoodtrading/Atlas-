Help & Glossary

The same handful of terms show up across every page in Atlas -- this is the one place they're all defined. Most pages also have their own "?" button next to the page title with page-specific detail (what's hardcoded, what the numbers on that particular page mean); this page is the shared vocabulary underneath all of them.
A2A (Amazon-to-Amazon)

Buy a product on a cheaper EU Amazon marketplace (Germany, France, Spain, or Italy), sell it on Amazon UK. The core sourcing model most of Atlas is built around -- Discovery, Products, Watchlist, and the Scan Queue all look for this spread by comparing a UK sale price against the cheapest of the 4 EU buy prices.

OA (Online Arbitrage)

Buy from a non-Amazon UK retailer, sell on Amazon UK. Unlike A2A, Atlas can't automatically discover an OA source from Keepa data alone -- see OA Lookup (manual, one ASIN at a time) and OA Source Discovery (automated batch search) for the two tools built for this.

Gated

A brand Atlas currently can't get Amazon's approval to sell, regardless of how good the numbers look. A gated product is still fully priced and tracked (see Gated Opportunities for the case it makes toward pursuing ungating) -- it's just blocked from the Review Queue, Discord notifications, and being auto-watched, since you can't act on it yet. Manage which brands are gated from Exclusions.

Excluded vs. Gated

Easy to conflate, genuinely different: Excluded means dropped permanently, never shown to you again anywhere, before Atlas even spends a token looking. Gated means still fully scored and tracked (so you can see whether it's worth chasing ungating) -- just blocked from the places that assume you can act on something right now.

Score vs. Confidence

Two separate 0-100 numbers shown side by side. Score answers "how good is this opportunity" (based on ROI, profit, sales velocity, competition, price stability). Confidence answers "how much should you trust that score" -- it starts at 100 and gets docked for things that make the underlying data less reliable: a big recent price swing, a sudden surge in competing offers, or thin sales evidence. A high score with low confidence is a real opportunity on paper that's riskier to trust than the score alone suggests.

Margin vs. ROI

Margin = profit ÷ the price you sold it for. ROI = profit ÷ what you paid for it. Both are shown together everywhere in Atlas and routinely differ a lot -- a product can have a great ROI (you paid very little for it) while still having a thin margin (the sale price itself isn't much higher than what everything -- fees, VAT, the item -- actually cost), which matters because a thin margin means less room for error if the price drops, an item gets returned, or a fee estimate is slightly off.

"Today" vs "90d" vs "Peak"

Every profit/ROI figure in Atlas comes in up to three flavours. Today uses the live current UK price. 90d uses the typical price over the last 90 days -- protects against a temporary discount making a normally-good product look bad (or a temporary spike making a bad one look good). Peak uses the highest price seen in the last 90 days -- only used for the riskier "PEAK (risky)" recommendation tier, since it only pays off if you can actually source and sell while the price happens to be at that high point, not on a normal day.

Sourcing tags (EU A2A / UK A2A / Wholesale (likely) / OA / unclear)

Shown on the Competitors page and Review Queue for competitor-detected listings. These are Atlas's best GUESS at how a competitor sourced something, inferred entirely from price history patterns -- never a confirmed fact, since Keepa has no visibility into anyone's actual purchase history. "Wholesale (likely)" and "OA / unclear" in particular are structural inferences (small stable seller count, recurring brand, multipack wording, or simply "nothing else explained it") rather than direct price evidence the way the two A2A tags are.

Match confidence tiers

Used on OA Source Discovery and OA Lookup to rank how sure Atlas is that a found retailer listing is really the same product, strongest to weakest: EAN match > Brand+MPN > MPN alone > Brand+Title text match > Title-only. Title-only is never treated as a confirmed match under any circumstances -- always verify it yourself before acting on it.

"Star buy"

Atlas's own bar for "worth a look": confirmed sales evidence (a real monthly-sold figure, or enough Keepa sales-rank drops to count as evidence) AND better than 25% ROI, today or on the 90-day-typical price. This is the exact rule (ProductRepository.is_notable) behind the Dashboard's star-buy alert, the green row-highlight on Discovery/Products/Watchlist/Review Queue/Replen, and what feeds the Review Queue from a normal scan.

"Ceiling" / "never viable"

A product is checked against Amazon's own UK fees (FBA fee + referral fee) BEFORE Atlas ever looks for an EU or UK source price. If the UK sale price alone can't cover Amazon's cut, no purchase price -- however low -- could ever make it profitable, so it's dropped immediately as "unprofitable (never viable)" to avoid wasting a token checking 4 EU marketplaces for nothing.

VA / SAS

Appears as a badge next to a profit/ROI figure on the Leads pages. It marks a figure that came from your own Virtual Assistant's row data (verified against your own sales tooling), not a Keepa estimate -- Atlas treats it as ground truth rather than recomputing or second-guessing it.
